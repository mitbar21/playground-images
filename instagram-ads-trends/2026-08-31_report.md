### Degraded input — hashtag discovery returned nothing usable

All 8 hashtag searches succeeded technically (240 results) but returned **zero videos** and near-zero engagement. Every result was an Image or Sidecar posted in the last 3 days, dominated by low-follower agency spam (`@jordanchrisjen`, `@itsyourdigital`, `@mypunchylime`, `@applabx.official` and similar), max engagement across the entire set was 21. `apify/instagram-hashtag-scraper` returns the raw recency feed, and for commercial ad hashtags that feed is almost entirely small agency promo posts.

**Consequence: zero breakout accounts were surfaced this run.** All 35 in-window videos, and therefore all 10 scripts, come from the tracked-account set. That is a discovery-layer limitation, not an absence of trend signal — the tracked set produced plenty. Worth flagging as a recurring pattern if it repeats next run.

## 5. Cross-account winning themes

Five themes repeated across accounts in this window.

**One, AI has moved from writing your copy to operating your ad account.** This is the clear breakout theme of the fortnight. Two separate creators posted it independently within a week, and the biggest post of the entire scan is exactly this idea, at 101K views with 4,162 comments. The shift viewers are reacting to is specific: not "AI helps you write ads" but "AI reads your account, identifies your winners, generates variations off them, and launches them back in." The comment-to-like ratio on that post is the strongest demand signal in the dataset.

**Two, ROAS is being openly attacked as the wrong metric.** Two reels in one week from the same account, both over 20K views, arguing that platform-attributed ROAS causes advertisers to kill the channel that actually created the demand. The replacement stack is consistent: blended CAC, MER, contribution margin, CAC-to-LTV. This is the most contrarian and most emotionally live idea in the set because it challenges the number most viewers report to their boss or client.

**Three, creator volume as an operating system rather than influencer casting.** Recruit dozens, give them a shared brief and visibility into what is currently winning, pay commission, then put paid spend behind the organic winners. Framed explicitly as a retention problem, not a discovery problem.

**Four, feed the algorithm better data instead of fighting it.** This is the strongest cross-platform theme and the only one that shows up on **both** Meta and Google. On Meta it is CRM status pushed back through CAPI so the performance goal can optimize for qualified leads rather than form fills. On Google it is the three-tier conversion model, where the fix for spammy leads is to change what you count as a conversion. Same mechanism, two platforms, two creators who have never referenced each other.

**Five, creative specificity beats creative polish.** The 1/10 vs 10/10 grading reel and the six-concepts reel both land on the same rule: broad category words are invisible, and an ad that opens on a problem the buyer already recognizes outperforms one that opens on a benefit list.

**Meta-vs-Google balance — the honest read.** This run is heavily Meta-skewed and there is no way to present it otherwise. Of 35 in-window videos, 30 are Meta or cross-channel and 5 are Google, and those 5 Google videos total **821 views combined**, less than one percent of the single top Meta reel. @aaronyoungofficial is the only Google voice in the tracked set, he has 2,933 followers, and Instagram is plainly a secondary channel for him. I pulled 2 of the 10 scripts from his content anyway because the ideas are good and the report should not be all Facebook, but they are **topic-balance picks, not reach-validated picks** — you should not expect them to perform like the Meta-sourced scripts on the strength of the source signal, only on the strength of the idea. If Google Ads is a real content pillar, the tracked set needs at least one more Google account with actual Instagram distribution.

## 7. Quick execution notes

**Record these four first.** Scripts 1, 2, 3 and 7 sit on the strongest source signal in the scan. Script 1 is the highest-confidence pick of the batch by a distance — the source did 101K views with over 4,000 comments, and it is the only reel in the dataset where comments outran likes. Script 2 is the most contrarian thing you can say to a paid-ads audience right now and it is defensible on the mechanism. Script 7 is the best pure teaching idea in the run, and it is the only one where you can genuinely claim the insight rather than report a platform change.

**Scripts 5 and 10 are a deliberate bet, not a data-backed one.** Both come from source videos with under 250 views. I included them so the batch is not entirely Facebook and because the ideas hold up independently, but you should judge their performance on their own and not read a flop as a failure of the Google pillar generally.

**On delivery.** Every source reel in this batch is a comment-trigger reel, and none of these scripts are, because no asset or keyword was supplied. If you have a real lead magnet and an existing DM keyword for any of these ten topics, say the word and I will rewrite that script as Template L with the comment close, which is demonstrably the mechanic driving the comment volume on the top-performing sources.

**On tone.** These are written for your on-camera register, not your written one. Let the sentences run together, land on the question hard, and do not smooth out the accusatory second-person openings — "you are still hand-building new ads every week" only works if it sounds like you are actually calling someone out. Do not read them word for word off a teleprompter. Take the claim and the mechanism, then talk.

**Pipeline notes for next run.** Two things to fix. First, `data/instagram-ads-cache/` has not refreshed since 2026-06-26, so the scheduled routine is not running and this was a full cold pull. Second, hashtag discovery is effectively dead for this niche — it returns only recency-sorted agency spam with no videos. Suggest either sorting hashtag results by engagement rather than recency, or dropping hashtag discovery in favour of scraping the follow-lists and comment sections of the top tracked accounts to surface breakout handles.
