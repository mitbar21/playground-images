### Winners should build the next winners (ML)

**Source video:** https://www.instagram.com/p/DcbRVHVxFLr/ — @realchasechappell
**Source structure breakdown:** Hook is a flat platform-news claim ("this new update is insane") that front-loads novelty before any explanation. Body walks a linear chain of consequence: connect the ad account to an AI model, it scans for top performers, it generates variations off those winners, it pushes them back live. Then it zooms out to the implication ("think about what this means") and reframes the whole thing as a self-feeding loop rather than a one-off tool. Close is a comment-trigger tied to a setup walkthrough.

**Cover (on-screen text):**

> Your winners should build themselves

**Script (spoken):**

You are still hand-building new ads every week off a spreadsheet of last month's winners. Meta opened up a connection that lets an AI model read your ad account directly. It pulls your top performing creative, spins variations off the exact concepts already converting, and pushes them back in live. One small setup, and your winners start feeding the next round instead of you guessing. Most of you will scroll past this and keep manually duplicating ad sets. Which one are you doing this week?

### ROAS is lying to you (V)

**Source video:** https://www.instagram.com/p/DcT05G4xdLB/ — @realchasechappell
**Source structure breakdown:** Hook names a single metric as actively dangerous, which is the contrarian turn. Body sets up the versus explicitly: platform ROAS on one side, a stack of blended metrics on the other. It proves the first side is broken with a scattered customer-journey example, then lists the replacement metrics rapid-fire. Reward is a reframed question ("stop asking which channel, start asking what happens to the business"). Close is a comment-trigger for a full breakdown.

**Cover (on-screen text):**

> ROAS is lying to you

**Script (spoken):**

ROAS is talking you into killing your best channel. It only reports what one platform claims it did. Someone sees your ad, watches a creator, searches you, and buys somewhere else. So you look at the dashboard, see a bad number, and shut off the thing that created the demand. Blended CAC, MER, contribution margin, repeat purchase rate. Those tell you what one more dollar does to the whole business. Optimizing for the bank account, not the dashboard. What did you turn off last month that was actually working?

### Stop paying one influencer (S)

**Source video:** https://www.instagram.com/p/Dcl32xeRouV/ — @realchasechappell
**Source structure breakdown:** Hook promises a behind-the-scenes look at a nine-figure brand's creator operation. Body immediately corrects a false assumption ("they didn't find one perfect influencer, they built a system"), then reveals the real bottleneck was retention, not recruitment. It walks the retention mechanics in sequence: shared space, content brief, visibility into winning videos, commission. Then it names the flywheel explicitly and stacks proof numbers. Close is a "stop X, build Y" command plus a comment-trigger.

**Cover (on-screen text):**

> Stop paying one influencer

**Script (spoken):**

Stop paying one influencer five figures and calling it a creator strategy. One person makes one video, it does nothing, and you decide creators do not work. The brands running away with this built a machine instead. Dozens of creators, a shared brief, a group chat where everyone sees which videos are winning right now, and commission so posting pays them. Winners become the example. The rest make variations. Then you put paid behind the ones that already proved out. How many creators are actually posting for you this month?

**Cover (on-screen text):**

> Ads people actually remember

Your creative is broad because you are selling the product instead of the outcome. Four angles that keep working. One, sell the result, not the spec. Took one before a long run, never cramped. Four, call out the behavior you are replacing. Your desk drawer is not a meal plan. Which one is missing from your account?

### Google is not the problem (ML)

**Source video:** https://www.instagram.com/p/Dcsmfb_Br6V/ — @aaronyoungofficial
**Source structure breakdown:** Hook is a direct pain question aimed at the exact sufferer ("spending on Google Ads and getting nothing but spammy leads"). Body immediately reassigns blame from the platform to the advertiser's own conversion data, then teaches a three-tier model: spammy form fills (high volume, low value), closed clients only (low volume, costs climb), and the middle zone. It lands on a decision rule for picking the middle tier. Close is a restatement that conversion data determines success, no CTA.

**Cover (on-screen text):**

> Google is not the problem

**Script (spoken):**

You are getting garbage leads from Google Ads and blaming Google. You fed it form fills. So it went and found you more people who fill out forms. That is the entire mechanism. Every conversion you send back teaches it what to hunt for. Send only closed deals and volume dries up and your costs climb. So pick the middle. The lowest quality lead your sales team would still happily take a call with, and feed that back as the conversion. What are you currently counting as a conversion?

A five out of ten adds a real person holding it and some proof numbers. Better, still missing the part that matters. A ten out of ten opens on the problem the buyer already knows they have, then hands them the fix. Which score is running in your account?

### Stop making mid-funnel ads (S)

**Source video:** https://www.instagram.com/p/DcMfxm2Ma9i/ — @sampiliero
**Source structure breakdown:** Hook promises a single change that beats competitors on creative strength. Body describes the common approach (building ads for every funnel stage) and names exactly where it breaks: it makes you believe you know what each person needs. Then it prescribes the inversion in strict order, top of funnel first, bottom second, middle never, and explains that the middle emerges naturally from creative volume and overlap. Close is a comment-trigger for a written document.

**Cover (on-screen text):**

> Stop making mid-funnel ads

**Script (spoken):**

Stop building ads for the middle of the funnel. You sat down and mapped out cold, warm, and ready to buy, then tried to write creative for each one. You do not know your buyer that precisely. Nobody does. Build top of funnel only. Problem, solution, who it is for. Then build bottom of funnel only. Kill the objection that stops the purchase. Run enough volume and the middle builds itself. That is where your hit rate comes from. How many mid funnel ads are you babysitting right now?

### Your lead ads attract junk (ML)

**Source video:** https://www.instagram.com/p/DcU7USfp0r1/ — @growzutrue
**Source structure breakdown:** Hook is an accusatory certainty claim ("I know for a fact you are not doing this one thing"). Body names the lever immediately (switch the performance goal to qualified leads) then backs up to the prerequisite: the platform cannot see lead quality unless your CRM pushes status changes back into the dataset. It walks the connection steps in order, then shows an AI booking flow updating the status and closing the loop automatically. Close is a flat statement of what was just taught, no CTA.

**Cover (on-screen text):**

> Your lead ads attract junk

**Script (spoken):**

Your lead ads are pulling junk because you told the algorithm junk counts as a win. Instant forms optimize for the fill. That is all the platform sees. It has no idea whether that person booked or bought. So connect your CRM back to your ad account and push the status changes in. Booked. Won. Lost. Then switch the performance goal from maximum leads to maximum qualified leads and point it at that data. Now it goes hunting for people like the ones who closed. Is your CRM even connected?

### Why good agencies still churn (M)

**Source video:** https://www.instagram.com/p/DchttGrpIa6/ — @growzutrue
**Source structure breakdown:** Hook promises the reason clients never leave, framed against the abundance of alternatives. Body is a stacked list of retention mechanisms delivered as a rant rather than a clean enumeration: branded client-facing app instead of the generic white-label portal everyone uses, automated lead nurture so leads never sit cold, and status updates that feed conversion data back to the platform. Reward is the argument that ingrained systems make leaving expensive. Close is a blunt warning that ad skill alone will not stop churn.

**Cover (on-screen text):**

> Why good agencies still churn

**Script (spoken):**

You can run great ads and still lose the client. Three reasons they leave. One, they log into the same white label portal every agency hands out, so nothing feels different. Two, the leads land and nobody touches them for two days, so your good leads look like bad leads. Three, they never update lead status, so the algorithm never learns who was worth having. Fix the follow up and the feedback loop and leaving gets expensive for them. Why would your client stay if a cheaper offer landed tomorrow?

### Your search ads look wrong (T)

**Source video:** https://www.instagram.com/p/DcPs2zgh1VW/ — @aaronyoungofficial
**Source structure breakdown:** Interview-format hook asking him to explain what changes for traditional search in AI surfaces. Body establishes the familiar model first, the relevance triangle of search term, ad copy, and landing page, and notes Google rewarded alignment with visibility and cheaper clicks. Then it introduces the disruption: a fourth element, AI generated content, now sits on the page alongside the ads. Lands on the consequence that ads must feel native to that context or they read as pure interruption and get ignored. No CTA.

**Cover (on-screen text):**

> Your search ads look wrong

**Script (spoken):**

Your search ads are about to sit right next to an AI generated answer, and most of them read like an interruption. Three things to fix. One, match the language of the question being asked, not the keyword you bid on. Two, make the ad answer something, not just announce a brand. Three, send them to a page that continues that answer instead of restarting the pitch. Relevance used to be a triangle. There is a fourth corner now. Have you looked at what your ads sit beside?
